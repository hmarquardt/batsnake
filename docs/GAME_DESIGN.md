# Game design

## Fantasy

Batsnake is a two-sided natural-history encounter. The bat fantasy is speed under incomplete information: make the cave briefly legible, spend that knowledge, then fly from memory. The boa fantasy is patience under constraint: interpret a moving thermal stream, select a promising ceiling position, lead one body, and accept the recovery cost of commitment.

Neither mode is a conventional first-person shooter. The bat cannot hover or strafe like a spaceship. The snake cannot walk or hitscan. Both animals solve the same chamber with incompatible senses.

## Bat run

The bat begins in the deep roost at the back of a departure stream. Forward momentum is persistent. Flaps add thrust and lift on a cadence and spend a regenerating reserve; gliding trades altitude for efficient motion; a dive produces the fastest run; spread wings brake and increase lift; climbing spends momentum; low speed creates a soft stall. Mouse steering rolls the view and receives more authority at useful airspeed.

The composed route passes the hanging shelf, the Narrows snake, a central occluding column, the Column snake, a second column/alternate lane, and the Moon Run snake before the entrance. The moonlit mouth is always the strategic direction, but columns and changing corridor center prevent a straight safe line.

An echo call reveals geometry only while its world wave crosses it. Calls also alert nearby snakes, so perception has ecological cost. Collision, capture, and escape terminate or score the run. Current scoring emphasizes progress and clean flight, rewards surviving the three ambush zones, and subtracts for collisions and calls.

## Snake hunt

Three anchors overlap different traffic segments. Q/E does not teleport an animal; it transfers the player’s observational point to another member of a loose hunting group. Each anchor offers a different sightline and travel prediction. A/D shifts the suspended body, W/S changes reach, and mouse aim remains constrained by the coils.

Holding strike tensions muscle and slightly opens the jaw. Releasing commits to a rapid, finite lunge. The head travels through world space and tests a swept volume, then captures or recoils through a cooldown. More charge adds reach but costs more recovery. Four player captures before the 68-second stream ends completes the hunt.

Companion snakes orient toward nearby bats, grow more alert after echo calls, decide independently, strike infrequently, and miss. Their strikes remove flock bats and reshape local traffic; they do not award player captures.

## Shared ecology

Flock agents prefer a longitudinal departure route but carry individual speed, height, and phase. Local separation prevents clumping. Nearby animals, the player, alert snakes, strikes, and echolocation perturb their route. Panic increases speed and spreads indirectly through changed separation pressure. Recycled agents sustain a readable stream for the slice without pretending to simulate a full colony.

The core dramatic cycle is reciprocal: the bat calls to learn, the call alerts the snakes, a posture changes, flock traffic bends, and both player types gain or lose an opportunity.

## Difficulty and progression

The vertical slice has one authored difficulty. Future difficulty should adjust sensory cooldown/range, flight reserve recovery, strike recovery, AI aggression/noise, stream density, and route variance—not merely animal speed. Potential progression is documentary rather than power fantasy: new caves, seasonal colony states, weather/entrance light, different anchor anatomy, conservation field notes, scored observation challenges, and additional predator/prey perspectives.

Future levels should preserve one readable ecological idea each: a flooded chamber with reflection-confused echoes, a constricted nursery with lower-speed flight, a storm-driven mass departure, and a sparse late-season stream emphasizing patient snake selection.
