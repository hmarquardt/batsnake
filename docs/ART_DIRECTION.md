# Art direction

## Thesis

Premium wildlife documentary meets restrained survival horror. The camera observes credible animal effort in a deep tropical limestone cave. The cave is ancient, wet, and occupied; darkness is composed negative space, not an absence of art.

## Palette and lighting

Normal vision uses near-black green stone (`#07100f`), muted wet teal, brown-black guano, and a narrow cool moon source. Warm colors are reserved for living heat, impacts, and rare biological information. No arbitrary fill lights are placed down the tunnel. Entrance bounce falls off quickly, silhouettes dominate the roost, and wet highlights are localized.

Use ACES filmic tone mapping, restrained exposure, modest bloom, exponential depth haze, and capped contrast. High quality can deepen shadow resolution and sensory persistence, not turn the chamber glossy. The entrance should read as air and direction before it reads as a white disk.

## Materials

Limestone is high roughness with local clear-coated wetness, pale mineral edges, darker seams, and scale variation across meters. Rock kits need silhouette breakup at large and medium scales; micro-normal maps cannot rescue repeated boulders. Guano is matte, accumulated in plausible roost footprints, and never used as gross-out decoration. Mineral stains follow water paths. Debris settles under formations.

## Sensory appearance

Echolocation is cool green-white geometry memory: a world-space moving pressure band, sparse hard returns, grazing contours, and a rapidly dying trace. It must never become a permanent outline mode or general exposure boost. Reduced flashing lowers band contrast and future reflection flashes without altering spatial range.

Thermal perception is biological, not a surveillance filter. Cave stone approaches cool black. Bat torsos and wing roots are creamy amber; thinner membranes are dimmer red-brown; distance, occlusion, and haze still matter. Wakes are faint evidence of velocity, not neon trails. A strike can smear heat briefly, while normal depth remains intact.

## UI

The world owns nearly the full frame. Typography borrows from measured field-documentary titles: a serif display face and small, tracked system labels using platform fonts, so no font network request exists. HUD elements hug edges, use hairlines and short phrases, and disappear on menus/outcomes. Avoid dashboard panels, minimaps, large health bars, rarity colors, loot language, and arcade combo treatment.

## Animation

Bat motion begins in shoulder and finger structure; membranes lag, gliding simplifies the silhouette, banking deforms asymmetrically, and panic adds cadence rather than random jitter. Boa motion communicates weight through delayed curves, subtle breathing, anchor compression, anticipatory head stillness, explosive extension, and long recoil. Camera motion supports embodiment but remains smaller and lower-frequency than creature animation.

## Prohibited clichés

No fantasy crystals, fluorescent cave plants, cyberpunk blue/magenta washes, arbitrary colored point lights, uniform wall brightness, excessive bloom, gore splashes, haunted-house props, rubber tube snakes, paper-cutout bats, spaceship controls, or full-screen red damage filters. Fear comes from incomplete perception, living traffic, occlusion, and commitment.

## Milestone 2 application

The chamber keeps its layout but gains deterministic limestone pore/roughness breakup, pale mineral runoff, selected wet surfaces, deeper entrance haze, and black vegetation silhouettes. The cave-mouth disk is radially feathered so the final approach reads as humid exterior air rather than a UI beacon. Major echo geometry deliberately separates cool mineral memory from dense amber-organic boa returns.

First-person anatomy is peripheral: bat membranes enter during effort and asymmetry, while boa cheeks and pit rows remain below the hunting line. Neither rig is allowed to occupy the aiming center continuously. Thermal adaptation suppresses normal moon/bounce lighting as warm emitters clarify, preserving black rock and spatial depth instead of applying a red/blue screen grade.

## Living behavior and information

Variation is staged as documentary rhythm, not wave UI. Stillness uses negative space and isolated sound; peak emergence thickens silhouettes, wing noise, mist motion, and interrupted thermal tracks. Route corridors are never drawn. Airflow and traffic give the mouth directional presence without turning moonlight into a quest marker.

Onboarding and reports use the field-station typography already present. Prompts sit outside the aiming center and describe bodily action, not key trivia alone. Seeds are specimen/session labels. Difficulty names preserve the natural-history tone. Gamepad labels replace keyboard labels in place; no controller diagram overlays the world.

The production hero bat is deliberately subordinate to art direction: the torso stays below the aiming center, and articulated membranes enter the lower periphery through effort. Fur, dark detail, and translucent membrane materials remain restrained in normal vision; six regional proxies preserve head/torso/root/membrane heat hierarchy. The procedural fallback follows the same peripheral rule.

Milestone 4 cave detail is navigational, not decorative scatter. Three formation profiles, displaced rock silhouettes, wet/dry material families, runoff, guano beds, and entrance vegetation are deterministic and instanced. The Narrows, Column, and Moon Run posts receive distinct nearby silhouettes while the established flight lanes remain open. Echo travel-front banding on faceted surfaces is the most visible unresolved rendering artifact.
